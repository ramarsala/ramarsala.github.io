Earlier this year, I had the amazing opportunity to travel to Japan! As someone deeply passionate about rhythm gaming, computer engineering, and Japanese culture, visiting Japan had long been at the very top of my bucket list. The trip was an unforgettable blend of world-class arcade gaming, breathtaking historical sites, incredible food, and admiring some of the most seamless urban infrastructure in the world.

Here is a quick retrospective of the trip and some of my favorite highlights!

### The Arcade Centers: Akihabara, Shinjuku, and Beyond

As an avid rhythm gamer, stepping into multi-story game centers like Taito Station, GiGO, and Round 1 in Tokyo (especially in Akihabara and Shinjuku) was surreal. In North America, finding dedicated rhythm game cabinets often requires a dedicated road trip. In Tokyo, you can step into an arcade and find entire floors dedicated exclusively to rhythm gaming:

- **Immaculate Maintenance:** Seeing row upon row of *beatmania IIDX LIGHTNING MODEL*, *SOUND VOLTEX Valkyrie Model*, *Chunithm*, *maimai DX*, and *Taiko no Tatsujin* cabinets maintained in immaculate condition was incredible. The buttons, turntables, touch sliders, and high-refresh-rate displays were perfectly tuned.
- **Top-Tier Community & Spectating:** Watching local Japanese players effortlessly clear Level 12 IIDX charts and Level 20 SDVX tracks with near-perfect accuracy in person was nothing short of inspiring. Despite language differences, the arcade etiquette and mutual respect among players made it easy to connect and share enthusiastic thumbs-ups between sets.
- **Card Readers & Network Features:** Using an e-amusement pass and Aime card directly on modern networked cabinets and experiencing the latest event updates, unlock events, and audio settings firsthand was a treat.

### Cultural Exploration: Kyoto and Historic Tokyo

Beyond the neon lights of Tokyo's tech districts, traveling through Japan offered a wonderful appreciation for history and tradition:
- **Kyoto's Temples and Shrines:** We took the Tokaido Shinkansen bullet train to Kyoto, where the atmosphere shifted from hyper-modern to serene and historic. Walking through the thousands of vermilion torii gates at Fushimi Inari-taisha early in the morning was one of the most memorable parts of the trip. Exploring Kiyomizu-dera and the quiet streets of Gion offered a fantastic perspective on Japan's architectural heritage.
- **Tokyo Neighborhoods:** Exploring the historic grounds of Senso-ji in Asakusa, walking through the vibrant pedestrian scramble in Shibuya, and discovering quiet residential alleys and gardens tucked right beside bustling metropolitan avenues gave a deep appreciation for the city's balance of energy and calm.

### Transit and Engineering

Coming from an electrical and computer engineering perspective, I couldn't help but marvel at Japan's public transportation network:
- The **Shinkansen (bullet train)** was an incredible engineering feat to experience in person—whisper-quiet, effortlessly traveling at nearly 300 km/h, and arriving down to the exact second.
- Navigating the Tokyo subway and JR Yamanote line with a digital IC card on a phone was intuitive and seamless. The precision, reliability, and sheer throughput of the transit infrastructure were remarkable.

### Unmatched Food

Naturally, the food was a constant highlight throughout the journey. Highlights included:
- Steaming bowls of authentic tonkotsu and shoyu ramen from small ticket-machine ramen shops tucked beneath train stations.
- Fresh conveyor belt sushi and sashimi with freshly grated wasabi.
- Authentic Kansai-style okonomiyaki and piping-hot takoyaki in Kyoto and Osaka.
- Endless convenience store (konbini) snacks, canned boss coffees, and matcha soft serve on long walking days.

### Final Thoughts

Visiting Japan was everything I hoped it would be and more. It gave me renewed motivation for my personal rhythm game goals, provided a deep appreciation for a beautiful country and its culture, and left me with memories I'll cherish for a lifetime. I definitely look forward to returning in the future!
