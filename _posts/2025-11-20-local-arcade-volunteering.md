Outside of research, engineering, and software projects, one of my favorite ways to spend free time is at the arcade playing rhythm games—especially Bemani and Sega titles like *beatmania IIDX*, *Sound Voltex*, *Chunithm*, and *maimai*. Over the years, the local arcade scene has become much more than just a place to play games; it is a close-knit, passionate community of players, artists, and competitors who share a genuine love for the hobby.

Recently, I've had the rewarding opportunity to give back to this community by volunteering at our local arcade, helping keep machines running smoothly and supporting local events.

### Bridging Electrical Engineering and Arcade Hardware

Rhythm games place extreme mechanical and electrical demands on arcade hardware. When players are hitting buttons at 10 to 20 inputs per second or executing high-speed scratch and slider motions, even microscopic discrepancies in switch actuation force or input latency become immediately noticeable.

Volunteering has allowed me to apply my electrical engineering background to real-world arcade hardware maintenance:
- **Microswitch & Spring Tuning:** Rhythm game pushbuttons rely on sensitive microswitches (such as Omron microswitches) paired with custom spring weights (typically 20g–50g). Over hundreds of thousands of plays, switches wear down or develop chatter, and springs fatigue. I've assisted in disassembling button assemblies, replacing degraded microswitches, cleaning optical sensors, and tuning button weights to ensure consistent, crisp actuation across all lanes.
- **Sensor Calibration & I/O Debugging:** For games with specialized input interfaces—such as optical encoders for turntable wheels, infrared sensor arrays, and analog sliders—regular recalibration is vital. I've helped test signal continuity, inspect wiring harnesses, and debug I/O interface boards to eliminate dropped inputs and false triggers.
- **Display Lag & Audio Synchronization:** Precise audio-visual synchronization is critical for competitive rhythm gaming. I've helped verify display refresh rates, test HDMI/DVI conversion lag on modern LCD retrofits, and balance audio amplifier levels so that game audio remains crisp even in a bustling arcade environment.

### Hosting Tournaments and Community Meetups

In addition to hardware maintenance, I've been actively involved with community organizing and tournament support. Local arcades serve as vital community hubs, and organizing regular gatherings keeps the community connected and vibrant.

Some of the events I've helped organize and support include:
- **Community Tournaments:** Setting up double-elimination and Swiss-style tournament brackets, establishing fair song-selection rulesets and difficulty tier divisions, and coordinating score tracking for competitive matches.
- **Welcoming Newcomers:** Rhythm games can look intimidating to first-time players. One of the best parts of volunteering is walking casual players through how the machines work, explaining timing windows, and offering tips on reading notes and posture.
- **Livestream Setup & Hardware Checks:** Coordinating capture cards, audio splitters, and camera angles for tournament streams so players and spectators who cannot attend in person can follow along with the action.

### Why It Matters

Arcades provide a unique "third place" where people from diverse backgrounds come together over shared passion and friendly competition. Helping maintain these machines and supporting events has been an incredibly fulfilling experience. There is something deeply satisfying about diagnosing a finicky piece of hardware, fixing it with a soldering iron or fresh components, and seeing players smile as they set new personal best scores on a machine that feels brand new again.

I look forward to continuing my volunteer work and helping our local scene thrive!
